# iPhone interface

On iPhone the touch interface adapts to a compact layout in portrait and
landscape. It has been tested on the desktop with simulated phone screen sizes
and scripted input, but not yet as a signed build on a physical iPhone.

| Control | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Tools | Open the scrolling tool drawer. Choosing a tool closes it so you can touch the city. Tap Close tools to dismiss it. Terrain tools remain available while shaping a new city. |
| Inspect | Select tile inspection in a founded or loaded city, then touch and release a tile. |
| Menu | Open the City, View, Speed, Disasters, Reports and Help commands. |
| Details / Alerts | Expand the footer's full metrics and warnings. Less collapses it. The compact footer keeps treasury, date and the current tool visible. |
| Explore | Use the left movement pad and drag on the right to look. The right-side actions adapt to walking, driving or flying. Menu pauses movement; Resume continues. |

Reports stack columns and turn financial and industry tables into labeled rows.
All their numeric fields and actions still work. Scroll vertically to read
long reports; dialog titles and actions stay outside the body scroll. Expanded
status details also scroll on short screens to leave room for Explore. The docked
keyboard reduces the available height. Rotation closes the tool drawer and
cancels held gestures; Explore requires Resume after a display change.

The minimap is hidden on phone layouts to leave space for the city and touch
controls. Returning to an iPad-sized layout restores its saved preference.
Files, suspension recovery, construction gestures and hardware controls work as described in
the [iOS touch and Files guide](ipad.md). In Files, use **On My iPhone →
SC2D** to copy a city into its Documents folder before browsing.

The iOS preset includes both the iPhone and iPad device families. Testing on a
physical iPhone should cover real safe areas and keyboards, how the touch
controls feel, rotation, Files, background recovery and performance in
representative cities.
