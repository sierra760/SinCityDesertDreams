Sin City - Desert Dreams — Privacy
Updated November 12, 2026

The game
Sin City - Desert Dreams is developed by Sierra Burkhart / Bristlecone Artists LLC. Gameplay does not require an account. The app has no advertising, advertising identifier access, tracking SDK, in-app purchases or app-controlled analytics uploads. Casino funds and debts are simulated city money. They cannot be purchased with real money or redeemed.

On your device
Cities, city names, mayor names, settings and gameplay history are stored locally. Map configuration and map tiles may be cached locally. These files are not automatically uploaded to the developer. You can delete saved city files using the operating system's file manager, including the app's exposed Documents folder in iOS Files. Operating-system backups follow your Apple/iCloud settings. The separate in-game iCloud Saves feature is described below. Uninstalling removes the app's local data, but copies you exported or backed up may remain.

Sharing and importing
Sharing a city sends the file only through the destination you select in the system share sheet. It can include city and mayor names and the city's gameplay history. The destination's own privacy practices apply. Imported cities can also join your private iCloud save library when you play or save them with iCloud Saves enabled.

iCloud Saves
The current builds ship with iCloud Saves switched off, and nothing is sent to iCloud while it is off. The rest of this section describes the feature on an Apple build that includes it. On such a build, iCloud Saves is enabled by default and uses your signed-in Apple account. You can turn it off in Options and continue playing and saving locally. When enabled and available, the game automatically transfers city continuations, last manual saves and conflict copies to your private CloudKit database in iCloud and downloads them on your other devices. Changed foreground play is checkpointed about once a minute; local saving does not wait for the network. This feature uses Apple services, not a developer-operated save server, and does not require Game Center.

Transferred save files include city and mayor names, terrain and city layout, custom signs and street names, simulation and financial/gameplay history, and saved mod progress where present. Library metadata includes city/mayor names, game date, population, funds, save time, play/editing stage and format/schema versions. Sync also uses random app-specific installation, slot and revision identifiers, file hashes, conflict ancestry, a coarse platform label such as iPhone/iPad/Mac, and Apple's record/change metadata. It does not send your device's personal name or advertising identifier. The app keeps an account-identity hash locally to separate libraries; it does not put that hash or your local file paths in cloud record metadata.

Turning iCloud Saves off stops synchronization; it does not erase earlier iCloud copies. Deleting a local file or uninstalling the app does not guarantee deletion of cloud or exported copies. Cloud records have no automatic expiry in the game; unresolved conflict copies are kept so you can recover them. Apple's iCloud storage, account and privacy controls apply to cloud copies. Apple receives service requests and handles them under its own privacy practices. No claim of end-to-end encryption or a specific Apple retention period is made here.

Optional online terrain
Ordinary gameplay and included cities work offline. Opening real-world terrain requests a small settings file from sincity-maps.sierraburkhart.com (the publisher's host, which names the map and search providers), map tiles from OpenStreetMap, and selected terrain data from public Amazon S3-hosted Terrarium and ESA WorldCover datasets. While real-world terrain is selected, the game also checks about once a minute that the two terrain data hosts can be reached. Network services receive your IP address and request details; selected map areas can be inferred from tile coordinates. The app does not request GPS permission or read your device's current location. Coordinates you enter describe your chosen terrain area, which may be anywhere.

Place-name search on macOS and Windows uses the public Nominatim service at nominatim.openstreetmap.org, operated by the OpenStreetMap Foundation. Pressing Search sends the text you typed and your system language to it; with this built-in provider nothing is sent as you type. Place-name search on iOS does not use the public Nominatim endpoint. There, a configured autocomplete service receives partial search text after you pause typing, as well as your language preference. The prepared provider is Geoapify through a Cloudflare proxy. The proxy caches suggestions in memory for up to 15 minutes and persists only aggregate usage counters. Geoapify states that successful request information is generally retained for no longer than 24 hours for access control, usage statistics and API improvements. The proxy does not forward your IP address to Geoapify; Cloudflare receives the incoming network request. This provider is not switched on in the current builds. Dragging the map and entering coordinates remain available when search is disabled. The settings file can change providers or switch them off, and a provider it names may offer suggestions after you pause typing; the terrain chooser displays the active provider's attribution.

Online providers may keep access logs and request data under their own policies. The publisher's settings host serves one static file and may keep ordinary web access logs (network address, time and the file requested). No claim of zero server-side retention is made here.
